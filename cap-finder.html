Winter note — steel wheels lose caps

Swapping to winter steel wheels? The caps from your alloys usually don't transfer — steel wheels have a different center opening, and plenty of them run with no cap at all or a push-through style. Don't order summer caps for winter wheels.

Caps pop off all winter — snowbanks, ruts, car washes. Losing one in January is normal; losing the match is the problem. Measure the wheel opening (or a surviving cap) in Step 2 before you order a replacement.

Keep a spare. One extra cap in the trunk beats a bare hub all winter — and it gives you a master sample to measure from if another one goes missing.

Frequently asked questions

What center cap fits my car?

Center caps are sized to the wheel, not the car — the same model can wear different wheels with different caps. Pick your vehicle in Step 1 above for the commonly listed OEM size and part numbers, then measure your own cap or wheel to confirm before you buy.

How do I measure a center cap?

Use a caliper and record in millimetres. Measure the outer diameter across the face of the cap, then flip it over and measure the clip diameter across the tips of the two farthest opposing clips. The clip diameter is the number that has to match your wheel opening — it is what holds the cap on. Full steps: How to Measure.

What size is a Chevy Silverado center cap?

The 2014–2024 Silverado 1500 (and GMC Sierra 1500, Tahoe and Yukon) commonly takes an 83 mm (3.25 in) cap, OEM part numbers 22837060 and 20942002. Other years and wheels differ, so confirm by measuring your cap or wheel.

Can I use any center cap that fits the hole?

No — diameter is only part of it. The clip style, clip count and clip depth all have to match too, or the cap will not lock in and will pop off. Match the clip diameter and the part number molded on the back of your old cap first.

My center cap is lost — how do I find the size?

Measure the wheel opening directly with a caliper — that diameter is what the replacement cap's clip diameter must match. Or pop a surviving cap off another wheel and measure it instead; it is more reliable than the bare wheel.

How much should a center cap cost?

Typical ranges: most single caps run about $50 each, and rare full sets can reach $1,000. The exact price depends on the cap. Send us your fitment in Step 3 and we'll confirm the fit and quote one total before you pay anything.

Why a tool, not just a chart?

Charts flatten the truth: the same Honda badge wears a 69 mm cap on one wheel and something else on another; a Tesla's published sizes openly disagree with each other. The Cap Finder keeps those conflicts visible instead of hiding them, and it always lands you back on the one step that never lies — measuring your own cap or wheel. Spend the minute here, save the return shipping.

The 1 mm rule: center cap sizing is metric and tight. A cap 1 mm small won't lock; 1 mm big won't seat. If your measurement lands between two listed sizes, trust the measurement and match the clip diameter first — that's the number that actually holds the cap on.
