What a Center Cap Should Cost — Dealer, Aftermarket and Marketplace, Honestly

Fitment guide · Buying

By oemcentercaps.com (JTT Imports) — measured fitment guides for OEM center caps, hub caps and wheel caps.

Center caps are small parts with a wide price spread, and the spread is mostly about channel, not quality. Here is the honest picture, channel by channel, using the figures our research actually documents.

Dealer / OEM

The ceiling. Genuine caps ordered by part number fit exactly and match the factory finish — and dealer caps for GM trucks, as one documented example, are commonly cited at $35–$50 each. A set of four at dealer money is real money, and availability fades as models age out.

Aftermarket brands

Brands like Dorman reverse-engineer factory caps and sell them for less. On popular fitments the quality is genuinely good; on obscure ones it's uneven. The tell of a good aftermarket listing: it publishes real OD/ID dimensions and accepts returns. The tell of a bad one: it lists vehicle names and no numbers at all.

Marketplaces & specialists

eBay and specialist hubcap retailers carry new-old-stock and clean used factory caps — often the only source for 1990s–2000s wheels, and usually the lowest price for genuine parts. The discipline is inspection: part number, clip condition, both diameters, in that order — the full routine is in our buying-used guide.

Where money gets wasted

  • Buying a full set before test-fitting one cap.
  • Paying dealer prices for a cap whose part number is plentiful used.
  • Ordering by vehicle name and eating return shipping on a 1 mm miss.
