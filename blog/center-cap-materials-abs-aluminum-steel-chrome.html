Center Cap Materials — ABS, Aluminum, Stainless and Chrome-Plated, Compared

How-to · Care

By oemcentercaps.com (JTT Imports) — measured fitment guides for OEM center caps, hub caps and wheel caps.

Ask what a center cap is made of and you've asked how it will age. The documented material set is short — ABS plastic (the large majority of OEM caps), aluminum alloy, stainless steel, and chrome-plated steel or zinc — and each has a characteristic life and death.

ABS plastic — the default

Light, cheap to mold precisely (clips and all), immune to rust, and finished in any colour or printed emblem. Its weakness is age: UV and heat cycles embrittle it, and the clips — the thinnest part — go first. A fifteen-year-old ABS cap usually dies at the clips, not the face. In the cold it stays serviceable; in the salt belt it never corrodes. For most drivers it's the right material, which is why the factories chose it.

Aluminum alloy

Feels premium, machines beautifully, shrugs off clip fatigue (retention is often a ring or screws instead). It can corrode at the edges where coatings break, and it's the priciest of the set. Common on higher-end and performance wheels.

Stainless steel & chrome-plated metal

Stainless is the survivor: heavy, tough, corrosion-resistant. Chrome-plated steel or zinc is the glamorous one — mirror finish when new, and the most salt-vulnerable of the family once the plating is breached: pitting and peeling follow, fastest in winter-road country. The documented salt-belt guidance is blunt: coated metal and quality ABS survive road salt best.

Choosing a replacement

  • Match the original material where you can — weight and retention behaviour were designed together.
  • Salt belt? Favour quality ABS or coated metal over bargain chrome plate.
  • Whatever the material, the fitment numbers rule: OD, clip ID, depth, part number.
Material is the second question. The first is always size — the Cap Finder starts there.
