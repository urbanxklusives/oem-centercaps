Where to Buy Center Caps

Three channels sell center caps, and each is the right answer for somebody. Here's the honest comparison — then live search slots for the common sizes.

1. Dealer / OEM parts

Best for: guaranteed fit and finish on newer vehicles.

Genuine caps ordered by VIN or part number fit exactly and match the factory finish — at a price. Dealer caps for GM trucks, for example, are commonly cited in the $35–$50 each range. Availability fades on older models.

2. Aftermarket brands

Best for: value replacements for common applications.

Brands like Dorman reverse-engineer factory caps at lower prices. Quality is good on popular fitments, uneven on obscure ones. Buy where the listing publishes real OD/ID dimensions and accepts returns.

3. Marketplaces & specialists

Best for: discontinued, used and NOS caps.

eBay and specialist hubcap retailers carry new-old-stock and clean used factory caps you can't get anywhere else — the only real option for many 1990s–2000s wheels. Check seller feedback, photos of the cap's back (clips!), and the return policy.

Whichever channel you choose: order by part number and measured clip diameter, not by the vehicle name in the listing title. First order for a new wheel? One cap, test-fit, then buy the set.

Before you check out

  • Your measured OD, clip ID and clip depth match the listing's stated dimensions.
  • The part number matches the one molded into your old cap (or your VIN lookup).
  • Photos show the back of the cap — clips intact, correct count, spring ring present if your old cap had one.
  • The seller accepts returns. Even careful buyers miss occasionally; fitment data in listings is sometimes wrong.
  • You're buying the right quantity — caps are sold singly, in pairs and in sets of four, sometimes on the same page.
